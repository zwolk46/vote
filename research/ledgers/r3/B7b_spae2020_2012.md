### C2 Mean or median?
- First pass said: nothing for 2012 (for SPAE 2022 "The report does not say whether these are means or medians", line 219, line 300).
- Mark: confirmed
- Source: stewart2012; PDF p. 13 (printed p. 13), Section III; Table 1 rows "Average", "Standard deviation", "Standard error".
- Quote: "Average wait times are estimated by first recoding the response categories to the midpoint of the category (i.e., the “none at all”"
- Quote: "response is coded as zero minutes, “1-10” minutes is coded as 5 minutes, etc.)."
- Quote: "For respondents who waited more than an hour and answered the follow-up question, I use the actual estimate of waiting time, in minutes, for that respondent."
- Note: The paper's "average" is an arithmetic **mean** of midpoint-coded categories ("none at all" = 0, "1-10" = 5, and so on), with the respondent's own minutes for those over an hour and, for the few who did not answer the follow-up, the mean of those who did. The paper never reports a median. [M] Cross-check from Table 1: 2012: 0 x .373 + 5 x .318 + 20 x .184 + 45 x .086 + 110 x .039 = 13.4 (reported 13.3); 2008: 0 x .368 + 5 x .276 + 20 x .190 + 45 x .103 + 109 x .063 = 16.7 (reported 16.7); the 45 for "31 minutes-1 hour" is my assumed midpoint, and 110 and 109 are the follow-up means (C10). [M] A median can only be bracketed: in 2012 "Not at all" 37.3% plus "Less than 10 minutes" 31.8% = 69.1%, so the median is in the 1-10 minute band, under 10 minutes; the paper does not state a median. The Dec 2012 post's "average" is not defined; it is by the same author and survey, so probably a mean [I]. The SPAE 2020 report does not define "average" either (A4).
