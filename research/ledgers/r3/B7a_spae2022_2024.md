## Headline for the lead

1. **The discrepancy is settled.** The 5% (Election Day) and 7% (early) that the 2022 report's executive summary (p. 3) calls "2020" are the **2018** values. The report's own chart on p. 20 shows 2018 = 5% / 7% and 2020 = 14% / 22%. The p. 3 phrase "in 2020" is a mislabel (the same sentence says "the last midterm election"). The 2022 and 2024 reports then agree on 2020 (Election Day 14%; early 22% in the 2022 report, 21% in the 2024 report, a rounding-level difference; the 2020 report itself is not my source, the first report says it prints 14% and 22%; see B7a-05).
2. **The "six minutes / four minutes" average (2022 report p. 20) cannot be confirmed as minutes.** The sentence exists, says "average", and does not say mean or median. But no table or chart in either report gives wait time in minutes; the only wait metric either report measures is the **share waiting more than 30 minutes**; the executive summaries use "average wait times" as the lead-in to those shares; and 6 and 4 are exactly the two 2022 shares (6% Election Day, 4% early) plotted directly below the sentence. [I] This may be a percent-to-minutes slip. The first report's C = $3.80 / $2.53 (sections 4.6 and 5) rests on it.

## Found that the first pass missed

1. **No SPAE report here measures wait in minutes.** Both reports use one wait metric, the share waiting more than 30 minutes. The 2022 "six minutes / four minutes" sentence (p. 20) coincides with the 6% / 4% shares beside it. [I] If it is a unit slip, the first report's C of $3.80 / $2.53 (4.6, 5) has no source in the SPAE 2022 report. Not decided here (rule 8); the lead should treat it as unverified.
2. **2024 gives the race split: 13.0% whites, 13.1% African Americans, 13.6% Hispanics; no significant difference** (p. 18), against 8.2% vs 3.4% in 2022. It also gives state counts (five states above 20%; 22 states above 10%).
3. **The 2020 series was revised between the 2022 and 2024 reports** [I]: early 22% to 21% (a rounding of about 21.5, pixel-measured), three state bins (AR, FL, NM) lower, and the 2020 non-voter reason shares differ (line too long 5.0% vs about 3.1%).
4. **Both executive summaries use "average wait times" to introduce 30-minute shares**; the word "average" in these reports should not be read as a mean time. 2024 "decreased" is only against 2020: 2024 (11%, 15%) is above 2022 (6%, 4%).
5. **The 2022 report's 2018 comparison is Cooperative Election Study data, not SPAE** (chart note, p. 20); so a 2018-to-2022 comparison mixes surveys.
6. **Sample size.** True n is 10,200 in both years (subsample sums); "10,100" is carried in the confidence-interval tables (and, in the 2022 report, in the text of the methodological note). The tables use simple-random-sample margins on raw n, with no design effect.
7. **Both reports caution that vote validation was unfinished when written**, so "voters" include some non-voters; the 2022 non-voter share (16% reported vs 14.7% raw) implies weighted statistics, though the word "weight" appears nowhere.
8. **The 30-minute threshold** is the 2014 Presidential Commission's benchmark, so the SPAE measure is a performance benchmark and not a cost estimate; neither report converts time into dollars or gives the number of voters deterred by lines (only the stated "main reason" shares: 2.2% of non-voters in 2022; 1.8% in 2024).
