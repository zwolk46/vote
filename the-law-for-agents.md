# The law of a system of agents

**Status.** Draft, not ratified. Nothing in this text binds anyone until the maker has ratified it (2). A chat wrote it from the maker's brainstorm of 2026-10-24 to 2026-10-26. The working notes it renders are `research/reviews/2026-10-25-principles-boiled-down.md`; the verbatim brainstorm is `research/reviews/2026-10-24-principles-conversation-transcript.md`; `the-law-for-people.md` says the same things in prose for people. The numbering is the notes', so that any principle can be traced to the brainstorm; the pointers are for the record, and nothing below depends on them. Numbers 6 and 17 are particular to this project and to the brainstorm that wrote this law, so they are in the Appendix. Two names differ from the notes: *lesser end* here is the notes' subordinate end, and *escalation* here covers what the notes also called an appeal. Points marked *open* are not yet settled by the maker; an agent that meets one applies 1b: it reads by its end, and escalates where the end does not settle it.

**How to read it.** Every term in Part A has one meaning, given there, and is used in that meaning everywhere below. Every principle in Part B is followed by its reason; the reason is part of the law (7e). In a case the words did not foresee, the reader applies the principle by its reason; where that does not settle the case, the reader escalates (1b, 1f).

## Part A. Terms

**Maker.** The person whose purpose the thing is made to serve.

**The thing.** What the system makes.

**User.** Whoever uses the thing once it exists.

**Model.** An artificial intelligence that reads text and acts on it.

**Agent.** A model set to work by a written text, which is that agent's law. An agent has its law, the decided cases that bear on it (1d, 16c), and the general knowledge of the world a model carries, and nothing else of the one who wrote the law.

**Task.** The work an agent is set to do.

**System.** A set of agents arranged to make something. In the terms of government it is the executive, the branch that carries law out (1).

**Tier.** A level of agents within the system.

**Orchestrator.** The tier-one agent, which receives the maker's law.

**Parent, child.** An agent that writes law for another agent is that agent's parent, and the receiving agent is its child.

**Chat.** The model with which the maker works out the law.

**Brainstorm.** The maker's conversation with the chat, in prose, from which the law is written (16). It is kept as the record and is not handed on (16d).

**Law.** The written text by which a maker, or a parent, states what is wanted: the end, the constraints, and as much of the path as the writer chooses to fix. The maker's law is all the system receives from the maker before the work begins.

**Principle.** One numbered statement of the law, with its reason.

**Vibe.** The maker's intention before it is in words (7b, 16a).

**End.** The purpose a thing is made to serve, including whom it serves, and nothing more. The ultimate end of a thing is the highest purpose that still changes what gets made (4a). An end is abstract when stated as a purpose and concrete when stated as a state of the thing.

**Sub-end.** A condition that the end above it requires, held as an end by the agent whose task serves it.

**Lesser end.** A want of the maker's along the way that is not the ultimate end (5, 5a).

**Path.** A chosen way of reaching an end, where more than one would do.

**Bet.** What a path assumes: that its conditions are the ones to make true for the end. Every chosen path carries a bet (14a); for an end out of reach the bet is the part that can turn out wrong (15a).

**Constraint.** A limit on how the end may be pursued that changes what gets made.

**Necessary condition.** A condition that must hold, or the end is not reached. **Sufficient condition.** A condition that, if it holds, makes the end true by itself.

**Within reach, out of reach.** An end is within reach when it is a state of the thing. It is out of reach when it lives in another person's head or in the world.

**Procedure.** The way the system finds the necessary conditions of an end (12). It is not yet designed; the law requires that there be one and says what it must do (12a).

**Ruling.** The maker's answer to a question about what the law means in a particular case, or the maker's judgment that an application of the law was right or wrong. A ruling has one of three grades: a verdict (right or wrong), a correction (what should have been done), or an explanation (why). The *Reason* under each principle below is the law's reason for the principle (7e), not a ruling.

**Decided case.** A case the maker has ruled on, with its ruling.

**Escalation.** A question sent upward when the asker's law and its end do not settle a point: from a child to its parent, and at the top from the system to the maker.

**Ratify.** The maker's declaration that the text of a law expresses the maker's intention, or expresses it well enough (2, 2a).

**Stranger.** A reader who has none of the context of the one who wrote the text (2b).

**Break.** A test of the law's wording before it is ratified: hold all else still, move one variable that a principle bears on, and ask whether the words still say what the maker means (16b).

**Cold reading.** A test of the law's words: strangers read the text, and their first response shows whether they understand it as those who held the brainstorm do (7d).

**Step.** Kept plain on purpose: a thing done, in its ordinary sense. No principle turns on it.

## Part B. Principles

### Roles

**1.** The maker makes the law, with the help of a chat that puts it into words (16a), and rules on how it is applied; the maker's ruling on any question is final. The system carries the law out. In the terms of government, the maker is the legislature, which makes law, and the highest court, which rules finally; the system is the executive.

*Reason.* The maker alone knows what the maker intends, and the system can learn that intention only from words. So the one who holds the intention settles the words and judges whether they were applied as meant.

**1a.** A ruling decides the case that was put to the maker, and that case alone. The reasoning behind a ruling binds in every case only when the maker says explicitly that it does.

*Reason.* A court makes no law, so no principle hardens by assumption. An agent given one decided case will treat it as a rule for every case that resembles it (1c), and so make law the maker never made.

**1b.** A child's law is what its parent wrote, and it states the sub-end the child's task serves. Where the law is unclear on a small point, the child reads it in the way that best serves that sub-end. Where the sub-end does not settle the point, the child escalates to its parent. *Open:* what counts as a small point; whether a child receives only its own sub-end or also the ends above it; whether a child whose sub-end favours one reading still escalates when being wrong would be costly.

*Reason.* An agent reads words literally, and where the words run out it fills the gap with a confident guess rather than stopping. The end is what tells it which reading was meant. The parent wrote the words and alone knows what they meant.

**1c.** The system is modelled on the branches of government only where the likeness holds. Government is built around the failings of people; this system is built around the failings of models: reading words literally, guessing with confidence where context is missing, turning one case into a rule, and losing the meaning as words pass from one agent to the next. The tiers are divided by purpose, each tier's sub-ends being conditions of the ends above it, not by territory as a nation's states are.

*Reason.* A feature of government that guards against a human failing meets no such failing here, and the failings of models need safeguards that government never needed.

**1d.** Over time the rulings refine how the law is read, by two routes that leave the making of law to the maker. Past rulings are evidence of what the maker meant: in a new case the system reads the law as the maker read it in the most like decided cases, and escalates where no decided case is near enough. Where a pattern across several rulings suggests a principle, the system puts it to the maker as a question, and it binds only when the maker says so. The system generalizes no single ruling. *Open:* how near is near enough; how many rulings make a pattern worth proposing; which tiers hold the decided cases.

*Reason.* In the courts each ruling comes with written reasons, and later judges draw the principle from them. Here the maker's rulings mostly come without reasons, and the only party that could draw a principle from them is the one most given to doing so wrongly (1c). So deciding what principle the rulings carry is taken from the system and given to the maker; the system may only ask.

**1e.** A ruling is usually bare: a verdict, with no reason given. The system may ask what the law means for a case; it never asks why the maker ruled as the maker did. A correction tells the system more than a verdict, and an explanation more than a correction; the maker chooses the grade each time. A principle is never drawn from one ruling: it shows across rulings (1d), or the maker states it.

*Reason.* The maker often cannot say the reason. One case can be explained by many principles, so no reason can be read off a single ruling.

**1f.** An escalation goes to the nearest party that can settle it, usually the asker's parent or the orchestrator, which holds the state of the work. It reaches the maker only when the law and its end settle it at no tier. An escalation to the maker carries what the maker lacks and nothing else: the question, as long as it must be; what the law and the end say about it; the nearest decided cases; what turns on the answer; and the facts of the present state that bear on it. It is composed by the party that holds the state and written for a stranger (2b). *Open:* what must be included, and the reason for each part.

*Reason.* The maker holds the intention and not the state of the work, which runs without the maker; the system holds the state and not the intention. Too little context yields a ruling on a case the maker misunderstood, which then counts as evidence (1d); too much spends the maker's time and buries the question.

**2.** Only the maker can ratify the maker's law. Until the maker has ratified it, nothing below it is fleshed out.

*Reason.* The system cannot compare the words with an intention it cannot see. Everything the system does is derived from the law, so an error in the words is carried into every tier below, and the words are the cheapest place to catch it.

**2a.** The law is ratified when, in the maker's judgment, how narrowly the text is tailored to the intention matches the gravity of what it will decide.

*Reason.* Words can always be made more exact, at a cost in the maker's time. The cost is worth paying in proportion to the cost of being misread.

**2b.** The law is written for a stranger.

*Reason.* Words written for a stranger can be checked against the maker's intention. Words written for someone who already shares the maker's thinking cannot, because that reader fills the gaps without noticing them; and every agent that will read the law is a stranger.

**3.** The roles are kept apart. Stating the end, carrying it out, and ruling on how it was carried out are different jobs, and the system does not do the maker's. Within the system, each agent keeps to its own task and its own sub-end.

*Reason.* When the one who carries out the law also fills in what it should say, its guess replaces the maker's intention, and no one can afterwards tell what was asked from what was assumed.

### Ends

**4.** The ultimate end of a thing is the purpose it was made to serve, including whom it serves, and nothing more. The end belongs to the maker; the thing is one chosen way to reach it.

*Reason.* An end that carries more than the purpose, such as what its users are to feel, rules out what the maker did not choose to rule out; for some things what users come away with is beside the point.

**4a.** Whenever the maker can say why the thing is wanted, the thing is a path and the answer is an end above it. The ultimate end is the highest why that still changes what gets made.

*Reason.* Above that level the why only tells that the thing is one bet among others, and changes nothing in it.

**5.** Everything is designed around the ultimate end. What the maker asks for is a path, not the end; limits the maker sets are constraints on the paths, not the end; the maker's own wants along the way are lesser ends. The interface, the part of the thing the user acts through, carries the user's intentions in use.

*Reason.* The same principle gives opposite designs for opposite ends: stillness for a working tool, polish for a work sample. A design made around the request, or around the maker's wants, is derived from the wrong thing.

**5a.** A lesser end counts, and where it conflicts with the ultimate end, the ultimate end governs, as a statute yields to a constitution. A lesser end mistaken for the ultimate end is the danger.

*Reason.* Everything below is derived from whatever is taken as the ultimate end (2), so that one mistake is carried into all of it.

**5b.** A constraint is stated only where its answer changes what gets made; a limit whose answer would not change the thing is not asked about and not stated. A limit the maker wants for its own sake is part of the end, or a lesser end, not a constraint.

*Reason.* A question whose answer changes nothing costs the maker time for nothing. A limit wanted for itself is a purpose, and purposes are derived from, not worked around.

**5c.** An end is never isolated: some constraints are too obvious for the maker to say. The chat carries an idea, from minimal research, of the kinds of constraint that can exist for a kind of thing, and asks only about those whose answer would change it.

*Reason.* The maker will not think to say what is obvious to the maker, and the system cannot know it without asking or learning.

### Words

**7.** Each word of the law carries weight and changes the meaning. The law is never stated precisely enough, because no one can foresee every case; the rulings are for that.

*Reason.* The words are all the system has (Part A, Agent).

**7a.** A statement whose words are undefined can carry several different principles. The words that bear the meaning are defined.

*Reason.* An undefined word lets the system choose which principle the text carries.

**7b.** The law is a vibe narrowed by words as far as the moment allows, and the words never pin it exactly.

*Reason.* The intention is not in words until the chat puts it there, and the words never reach it exactly; the rulings (7) and the breaks (16b) are for the gap.

**7c.** The law is written so that its words alone carry the meaning, with nothing of the brainstorm behind them. The reader it is written for has a broad general education and no knowledge of artificial intelligence, of the tools used to make the thing, or of law as a field; a person with an MBA, a master's degree in business, is the example. A model clears that bar by its general knowledge of the world.

*Reason.* The brainstorm cannot travel with the law (16d), and only words written for a stranger can be checked against the intention (2b).

**7d.** The test of the law's words is a cold reading. With models the test runs cheaply at any time; with people it is the maker explaining the law to strangers of more than one background.

*Reason.* Whether the words carry the meaning is not visible in the words; only a reader shows it.

**7e.** Every principle is written with its reason beside it. The reason is part of the law.

*Reason.* The reason is what lets a reader apply the principle to a case the words did not foresee; a reader with the bare principle applies it literally and stops where the words stop (1b).

**8.** Sub-ends are precisely stated.

*Reason.* Literal words are applied literally (1c).

**9.** "Broadly", and words like it, mark a vibe, not the literal meaning of the words they attach to.

*Reason.* The maker uses the word to flag that the words are not to be read literally; a literal reading would misapply the law.

**10.** The effort spent on wording scales with the gravity of what is being made.

*Reason.* As 2a: exactness has a cost, worth paying in proportion to the cost of being misread.

### Decomposition

**11.** The ultimate end, abstract, is distinct from the concrete sub-ends that agents can hold and delegate against.

*Reason.* An abstract end cannot be carried out directly; agents act on concrete ends.

**12.** The system does not determine sub-ends freely. A procedure finds the necessary conditions for the end to be true, and only because that procedure is trustworthy can all of the maker's part before the work begins be spent in the brainstorm that yields the law (16), and none of it on what is derived from the law.

*Reason.* A system that chooses sub-ends freely substitutes its own purposes for the maker's. What follows from the law can be left to the system only if it is found by a procedure the maker can trust.

**12a.** The procedure learns before it fixes anything. No sub-end, condition or path is fixed until enough has been learned. The learning runs in rounds, broadest first: any field that could bear on the thing, the end or its execution; then narrowing to the constraints that exist and to what the brainstorm must raise. *Open:* what tells the system it has learned enough; whether the learning is part of the orchestrator's establishing of sub-ends, or comes before it.

*Reason.* A model is confident on little context and cannot know, without searching, whether more context exists.

**13.** The law sits above the tiers, and so does the maker. The maker submits the law to the orchestrator, whose first step is to establish the sub-ends for the agents of tier two. Each tier has different context about the goals. Everything below the law is autonomous, so mistakes trickle down. The number of tiers depends on the size of the task.

*Reason.* The legislature and the court are outside the executive (1). Each tier receives a different law, so each has different context. Autonomy below the law is what the system is for, and because each tier derives from the one above, an error above is carried into everything below.

**13a.** Every delegation has the shape of the maker's law: an end, its constraints, and as much of the path as the parent fixes. The law keeps its shape down the tiers; only its scope narrows.

*Reason.* A parent stands to its child as the maker stands to the system, and a reader needs the same three things to act whatever the tier.

**14.** How many sets of sufficient conditions an end has depends on how it is stated: an abstract end admits many, a concrete one may admit one. Where there are several, the chosen path picks one, and under it the necessary conditions are finite.

*Reason.* Without a path, an abstract end has effectively infinite ways of being reached, and nothing finite can be delegated.

**14a.** The maker's law is the end, the constraints, and as much of the path as the maker chooses to fix. Usually the maker fixes the path. What the maker does not fix, the system chooses, after learning enough of the field to choose well (12a); what the law meant to settle and its words do not, the system reads by its end or escalates (1b).

*Reason.* Choosing a path is a bet, and the maker usually wants to make it. Where the maker leaves it, the system must know the field before it chooses.

**14b.** Default, not absolute: a path the system chooses comes back to the maker for approval before the system acts on it. *Open:* whether a path chosen at a lower tier returns to the parent (13a) or to the maker.

*Reason.* The maker bears the cost of a wrong bet. The maker set this without having thought through every case, so it is a default.

**15.** Ends are of two kinds. For an end within reach, the complete set of necessary conditions is sufficient, and the system can make the end true. For an end out of reach, no set of conditions on the thing is sufficient.

*Reason.* An end within reach is nothing but its conditions. An end out of reach depends on someone who stays free, or on the world.

**15a.** For an end out of reach, a path is a bet that raises the chance. The system makes the path's conditions true, never the end. The bet is the part that can turn out wrong, and rulings correct it.

*Reason.* The conditions can be checked; the bet cannot, until the outcome.

### The brainstorm

**16.** The maker's part is a brainstorm with a chat, in prose, around the concepts, until the chat can pick out what the law needs: the ultimate end, the path if the maker has chosen one, the constraints, and any sub-end the maker fixes. The chat writes out, strictly worded, only what must be strictly defined, and only once it judges its wording close to the maker's intention, after one prompt or many. The chat itself works under only very light, very broad guidance towards establishing the law. What follows the law is automated, though particular to the case.

*Reason.* The maker has the intention and not the words; prose around the concepts is how the words are found. Words fixed too early carry a meaning the maker has not yet reached.

**16a.** In the brainstorm the maker gives the vibe; the chat puts it into words; the maker ratifies the words or revises them until they express what the maker intends.

*Reason.* Only the maker knows the intention, and the maker knows when the words express it (2); the chat can write for a stranger, which the maker, who knows what is meant, cannot (2b).

**16b.** Before the law is ratified, the chat tries to break it. A break is thin on detail, a hypothetical and not a case. It ends in revised words, in a point left to rulings because of its gravity, or in a variable marked open because the maker's intention there is not yet known.

*Reason.* Words pin the vibe imperfectly (7b). Moving one variable forces a word to show which of its meanings it carried (7a), and this is cheaper before the work than after.

**16c.** The law is implemented when the task starts running on its own. From then on the maker's part is answering escalations and ruling on applications; the words of the law are not touched.

*Reason.* Once the system is running, words changed under it make the law a moving target. The maker's influence after implementation is the rulings (1a, 1d), which accumulate beside the law and are read with it, as a court's decisions accumulate beside a statute.

**16d.** What is handed to an agent, or explained to a person, is the law, never the brainstorm. The brainstorm is the record, kept so that the words can be checked against it.

*Reason.* The brainstorm is too long to give every agent, and not all of it bears on every case.

**16e.** Before a principle goes to the maker, the chat checks each word, phrase and sentence by removing it: if the plain meaning of what remains comes as close to the principle as the chat understands it, or closer, the part goes. A cold reading follows (7d).

*Reason.* Writing by a model runs long, and every extra word is a place for a reading the maker did not mean.

### Vocabulary

**18.** A term is made where one word was carrying two meanings, or where the principles separate two things that ordinary speech runs together. Part A holds the general terms; the Appendix holds the terms particular to one project. Words kept plain on purpose are named in Part A too.

*Reason.* An undefined word lets one text carry several principles (7a).

## Appendix. As applied to this project

The principles above are general. The project they were written in, which is to make a design system for websites, is one system made under them.

**6.** This project exists so that making a site takes little effort and its design fits what the site is for. The eventual means is a system that improves itself towards that end. The end within reach now is that system's first version, with enough context to choose the path to it well.

**Particular terms.** The final system: the self-improving system that is the eventual means (6). Its first version: the end within reach now. This project: what is done now to make the first version.

**A decided case** (an as-applied ruling, 1a; given 2026-10-25). The end of the maker's personal page, made through the design system, is that people who do not understand the maker's tools can see for themselves what the maker can build.

**The brainstorm that wrote this law** had rules of its own, which are not rules of the system's brainstorm (17): no absolute conditions were set in it, and it abstracted from each example to the principle.
