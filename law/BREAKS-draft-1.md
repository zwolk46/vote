## 1. Breaks of intention

**#1. The unit of the claim.** Intention, **G**.
- *Variable:* what "one vote" is in draft 1: the choice in one race, or the ballot.
- *Case:* a general-election ballot carrying the presidency and nine other races. The presidency's p × B is far below the cost of the trip; the ballot's sum may not be.
- *The draft:* Part A, "A vote. The choice one ballot makes in one race. 'One vote' and 'an individual's vote', in the maker's words, mean this." Part A, "Cost ... to cast the ballot." Draft 1: "one vote's expected benefit, by itself, in most cases does not outweigh what it costs to cast." Draft 2c: the trip is judged over the ballot's sum.
- *His words:* "the difference between voting and not voting and still putting the thing into the machine" (the ballot); "an individual's vote the vote itself ... the expected benefit cannot possibly in any case probably outweigh the cost" (19:32, before the race-or-ballot question existed); ruled "Each race, and the ballot summed" (02:32). "One vote" is not his phrase: it is the orchestrator's at 19:34 ("your one vote changes the winner"); he said "a vote", "the vote itself", "an individual's vote" (#56).
- So draft 1 states his claim per race against a per-ballot cost, the comparison the ruling moved to the sum, and Part A tells him what his words mean. The end and the standard carry two units.
- *Fix:* in 1, "that what a voter casts is in most cases not expected to be worth what casting it costs, race by race and for the ballot as a whole (2, 2c)"; in Part A, "His 'a vote' and 'the vote itself' are read as this, with the ballot summed in 2c."

**#2. A verdict the standard cannot give, and the claim as the purpose.** Intention, **G**.
- *Variable:* whether the site has a B.
- *Case:* a House race with break-even stakes of $2 million. Is the benefit greater than the cost? Only with a B. Under 2 the site chooses none.
- *The draft:* 1, "It shows, for real US races, in which cases the benefit is greater than the cost and in which it is less." 1a, "including the races where a vote pays for itself." 2, "It does not choose whose welfare B counts."
- *His words:* "In which cases would the benefit be greater than something? In which cases would it be less? Although we don't necessarily have to do a find a number for it to be greater or less than, of course" (19:32); chose "Lead with break-even, show both" (00:24). He wants the cases sorted and does not require a number; the ruling gives the threshold.
- Without a B the site can show what the outcome would have to be worth, not whether the benefit is greater. 1 and 1a as written require a verdict per race that 2 forbids. And "so that the maker can show why he says an individual vote doesn't matter" makes his claim the purpose: a builder who reads it literally takes the choices that favour the claim (the voter-alone reading, the half at a tie, the full wage), which 1a and 9a only partly guard; and if the data tell against the claim the site has no end left.
- *Fix:* "1. The site exists so that the maker can show, for real US races, what one vote is expected to be worth against what casting it costs: the reasoning behind his saying that an individual vote doesn't matter. It shows, race by race, what the outcome would have to be worth for the vote to pay, so that a reader sees in which cases it may and in which it cannot." And 1a: "including the races where the break-even stakes are lowest, which tell against his claim."

## 2. Breaks of reading

**#21.** Reading, **W**. 2b: "the effect of a vote for one side against a vote for the other". A stranger asks: effect on what? *Fix:* "how much likelier one side is to win for a vote cast for it rather than for the other side, which is twice the gain over casting none".
