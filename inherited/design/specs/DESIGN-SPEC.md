## 0. The heart of what the maker does not want

His words of 13:08 UTC are "a LOT of text, and a lot of it looks the same", and he wants "more deliberate separation … (doesn't mean MORE, but deliberate)". On 13:17 UTC he called text that need not be read "a characteristic of AI-generated websites". On 13:21 UTC he called the bars "just rectangles".

On the built page, the cause is measurable (R s.2):

1. **Everything sits at one level.** The country view on desktop sets text in **16 combinations of size and weight**, with **8 sizes between 10.5 and 16.8 px**. Its largest text is a 16.8 px section heading. The seat counts, the most important figures on the page, are 14 px. A step of 1 px (13, 14, 15) is too small to read as rank [inferred], so eight sizes read as one size, and nothing leads.
2. **Figures are inside sentences.** "Hochul (D) 50.7% · Blakeman (R) 49.3%" is a sentence the eye must read from the left to find a number. In no list do the figures sit on a shared edge.
3. **Every row is the same shape.** The 35 Senate rows, called, close, not counting or not yet closed, share one form, and the state is told by the same weight as the office.
4. **Separation is uniform, so it is not deliberate.** Every row has a hairline. On the state and chamber views, every race is also a bordered, rounded box, so boxes and hairlines carry the same weight everywhere.
5. **Words repeat what the page already says.** "U.S. Senate" appears on each of 35 rows under the heading "Senate races". "Governors" appears twice on the phone (13:21). A source and time line appears under every card. The calls column has a three-line explanation. The footer names the typeface.

The fix is fewer levels, each clearly different, with figures on their own edge and one deliberate separator per kind of group. It is not more decoration.

## 1. Rules for every view

**R1. Text appears only if the viewer must read it to understand what is shown** (the maker, 13:17 UTC). *Bounds* (13:21 UTC): data is never extra text. The last election's result under a race stays, and so do credits that a law or licence requires, kept to what the requirement asks.
- **Goes:**
  - explanatory subtitles under headings ("Calls are civicAPI's decision desk's, with the time this site first saw each…");
  - instructions ("Rest the pointer on a state, or move to it with Tab…");
  - the typeface line;
  - "Candidates as printed on the state's official list: source." as a sentence (the link moves to the source line, R8);
  - "(civicAPI's own figure of precincts reporting; not a share of the expected vote)" (this becomes the meaning of the label, set once in the source line of the section where it applies);
  - the map credit about Hawaii's islands (s.10.2).
- **Stays:** the last election's result; the fixture banner (it must be read, or invented figures are taken for real ones); a stale or withdrawn notice; "paler: not up this year" under the Senate bar (without it the paler part cannot be read); the Census and civicAPI credits, once, at the foot, in one line each.
- **Test.** Cover the line and ask whether a viewer would now misread something shown. If not, the line goes.

**R2. Type: five sizes, two weights, four roles.** IBM Plex Sans (the plan).

| token | desktop | phone | used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--t-fig` | 32 px / 600 | 26 px / 600 | the seat counts only |
| `--t-head` | 24 px / 600 | 22 px / 600 | a view's title (a state's or a race's name) |
| `--t-sub` | 19 px / 600 | 19 px / 600 | section headings; the countdown in the header |
| `--t-body` | 15 px / 400 and 600 | 15 px | names, figures in lists, the body |
| `--t-src` | 12 px / 400 | 12 px | labels, statuses, axis labels, source lines |

- Each size is about 1.25 times the next, so each step reads as a rank [inferred]. Weights are 400 and 600 only. The font file holds 400 to 600, so the 700 the page asks for today (14 px / 700, R s.2) is synthesised or clamped.
- Line height is 1.4 for text and 1.05 for `--t-fig`. The two 11 px exceptions are the state codes on the map and on the chips, which are drawings, not text.
- *Reason:* 13:08 ("a lot of it looks the same"). Five clearly different levels replace eight close ones.

**R3. Figures.**
- Every figure in a column uses tabular numerals (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) and sits on a shared right edge.
- In a list, a figure is never inside a sentence. The leader's share is 600 weight and the others 400.
- A percentage keeps one decimal. A vote count, where shown, is 12 px muted beside the share.
- *Reason:* a column of figures on one edge is read down, not across. Comparing races is the room's task on the country view.

**R4. Colour roles.** (v1.22.1: a race's called colour now comes from the first standing call by civicAPI or any desk, s.33, on the maker's 00:13.)
- **Ink:** names and figures.
- **Muted:** labels and statuses ("21% in", "Dem", "to call").
- **Faint:** source lines and upcoming ticks.
- **Party colours only:**
  - the seat counts and bars;
  - a *called* winner's name, check mark and share, with the 3 px rule at the row's edge;
  - the timeline's call marks;
  - the map's fills.
- An uncalled leader is never in a party colour. *Reason:* colour then means "called", which is the event the room waits for.
- **The call's moment is drawn, not filled (v1.4.3, the orchestrator 20:05):** on a call the 3 px rule at the row's left edge is drawn in the party colour (MOTION-SPEC surface 18, `call`), and the name, check and share take the colour as it draws; nothing fills the row, and what stands is this rule's list. *Reason:* the motion moves what the design draws; a standing tint on every called row means nothing by midnight and boxes the row (R5).
- **One exception (v1.3.1, adopted by the orchestrator 15:47):** the county fills of the race-in-focus map (13.2) show who is **leading**, at an opacity from the share counted. Allowed because its legend says "leading" in words beside each colour, so the colour there cannot be read as a call; nowhere else does an uncalled leader take a colour. *Reason:* a county map with no fills until the call shows nothing all night in a close race (law 7: show what the source gives); the word in the legend keeps R4's meaning.
- **The band** (`--band`, W8 surface 11) marks only figures that changed recently.
- **Warn colour** marks only the fixture banner, a stale source and a withdrawn call.

**R5. Separation without boxes.** Use, in order of strength:
1. **Space.** The scale is 4, 8, 16, 24, 40 and 64 px. Sections are 40 px apart (32 px on the phone). A heading sits 16 px above its content.
2. **One rule per group.** A 1 px `--line-strong` rule opens a list under its heading. Rows inside a list are divided by a 1 px `--line` hairline. Nothing else draws lines.
3. **Alignment.** A list's figures share one edge, and its labels share another.
- No bordered or rounded boxes around races: the state view's race panels and the chamber views' card grid become sections with a heading and a rule (19:00 part). No shadows anywhere.
- The only raised surfaces are the phone's cards and sheets and the side menu, because they really lie above the page. Each has its own background and a 1 px `--line` edge; the cards also have 12 px top corners. None has a shadow.
- *Reason:* "deliberate separation … doesn't mean MORE" (13:08). Soft shadows between cards are a named fault.

**R6. A heading is said once.** A section's heading is not repeated by its first row, its tab or the view's title. For example, the Senate list's rows say "Alabama", not "Alabama · U.S. Senate", and the phone's Governors page says "Governors" once. *Reason:* 13:21 (7).

**R7. The source line, once per section.** Use one line at the foot of a section, 12 px faint: "Results: civicAPI. Called by civicAPI's decision desk; times are when this site first saw each call." Repeat it per race only where a race's source differs from its section's. The race's own "as of" time is data and stays with the race (W8 surface 20). *Reason:* R1. It is the same words 35 times.

**R8. Statuses in words, short.** "21% in", "Called 9:27 PM", "Polls close 11 PM", "No votes yet", "Call withdrawn", "No new count since 9:04". The zone is never in a status ("Polls close 11 PM", not "… 11 PM ET"): the header says it once (v1.8.1, from the check s.31, where "ET" made the phrase wrap at 390). **A status is the phrase in the status place** of a row, a cell or a card (v1.8.3, W9's question); a sentence (the header's "Polls close next at 11 PM ET", the card's "Polls close 1 AM ET in part of the state") and a group head in the calls list keep the zone where the sentence needs it, said once in each. Each is 12 px muted, or warn for the last two, in the same place on every row. *Reason:* the status is the second thing the room reads for a race. One place and one length make the column scannable.

**R9. Real icons, real examples.** One icon set, Phosphor Regular, every glyph named in s.19; no key legend, hint row or ornament of the generated kind; every component cites the real example it is designed from (a Mobbin screen or a details.so item). *Reason:* the maker, 21:20 (RULINGS.md); the whole of it is s.19. **(v1.17.1, 21:13 UTC, from the dark pass, research s.64: a glyph is drawn in the text's colour, in both schemes.** The pin, the pinned mark and the grip were SVG background images with no fill, so black in both schemes: at 0.75 over the light page they read, over the dark page they vanished (the maker, 20:53: "in dark mode, the pin icon is black, so it can barely be seen"). **9.1 The library: Cupertino Pane.** `cupertino-pane` **1.4.21**, Roman Antonov, licence **MIT**, plain JavaScript with no framework layer, its own motion and drag [stated: its `package.json` and README, read by W11; W11's `s-cupertino.jsx` on probe/components]. Rule 12 as written for drawers: the contents are mounted before the press and kept out of view; W8's gallery measure gave Cupertino one gap of 60 ms at 4× and no stall (his s.50), and he measures the build.

**9.2 Halfway by default; full only by the test.** The pane opens at the **middle break at half the screen** (`breaks.middle.height = round(innerHeight × 0.5)`, `initialBreak: 'middle'`; s.9's 55% goes with the card). **The half is a ceiling, not a floor:** when the content is shorter than the half, the pane's height is the content plus the grip and a 16 px foot (research s.43's rule, where all nine sheets showed 165 to 218 px of blank under a short card), so nothing empty stands under the last line. **It opens at the top break (full height, the site's 26 px top inset) only when the test says so:** (a) the content's first screen, laid out, does not fit the half less the grip and foot; or (b) the half would hide the thing the press was for (the control or figure named by the press lies below the half). Where the test is met the top break is enabled and the pane starts there; where it is not, the top break is enabled only if the content is taller than the half, so a drag up reaches the rest, and the pane starts at the half. **Applied (v1.13.1; the orchestrator, 13:06):** both of the site's drawers open **halfway**. The calls sheet's list runs newest first, so the half screen carries the calls a press on "All calls" asks for and a drag up gives the rest; the night card's timeline and closing list fit a half the same way. **Full is the exception,** for a press whose object the half would hide (a single race's detail, if one ever opens in a sheet; (b)), or a first screen that cannot fit the half at all ((a); none today). *Reason:* the maker's 13:00 makes halfway the rule and full the exception, and "more relevant information" is what the half shows for a list, not what it hides below (W5's v1.13 reading of the calls sheet as full, from its length, was the wrong side of his test). *Reason:* his words ("open it halfway" where full is not needed "to show more relevant information"); the half keeps the page in view behind it, which is what a sheet is for.

**9.3 The scrim.** `backdrop: true`, `backdropOpacity: 0.4` (the site's cover, as the reveal's 21.3), a tap on it closes (`onBackdropTap` → `destroy({ animate: true })`); the page behind is never scaled or moved (vestibular, surface 10's reason); the page is inert while the pane is open (`inert` on `main` and the header, applied when the opening has settled, rule 12's drawer clause), and `overscroll-behavior: none` on the body while open, `auto` restored on close (the library sets it; the site restores it: research s.43).

**9.4 The grip and the look.** `showDraggable: true`, styled as the site's: a 36 × 4 grip in `--line-strong` centred 8 px from the top, 12 px corners, the page's background, no shadow beyond the library's (W11 reads its default and matches the dock's 0.4 cover with no second shadow), the site's type; the library's own close button off (`buttonDestroy: false`). **No "‹ Back" and no × in the head:** the grip's drag, the scrim's tap, Escape and the browser's back are the ways out, and one kind of control one way (P7). The 13:21 ruling's "a back button on a state's view" is met by the page's title row (s.22), not here.

**9.5 Drag, close and back.** The pane follows the finger (the library's own), `bottomClose: true` and `fastSwipeClose: true` (a flick closes); a slow drag closes past half the way down, and a 60 px drag **snaps back** (research s.43 found 1.4.21 closing on a 60 px slow drag, too eager: W11 reads the library's source for its close threshold and sets or patches it so that a short drag snaps back, or records that it cannot and says so). **Back:** each opening pushes one history entry (`?sheet=` or the state W11's `shist.js` guard uses), the browser's back and the phone's back gesture close it, and closing by any other way pops that entry, so the stack is as the reader built it and back never leaves the site (law 7: the control does what it says). **Keyboard:** Escape closes; focus moves into the pane on open and back to the pressed control on close; the pane is `role="dialog"` with `aria-label` the thing it holds.

**9.6 Reduced motion.** `present({ animate: false })` and `destroy({ animate: false })`: the pane is at its break, and gone, at once; a drag still follows the finger (direct manipulation is not motion that reduced motion removes, WebKit) and settles at once on release; the scrim changes at once. The library has no reduced-motion handling of its own, so this is the site's.

**9.7 Motion figures.** The pane's own curve and lengths for the rise and the settle are the library's; W8 writes them into surfaces 6 and 32 P as measured on the build (his to confirm; the site's `sheet` token, 500 open / 350 close, is the reference he compares against).
