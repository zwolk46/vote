# What every worker brief shares (cited by each brief; read whole)

*Written by the orchestrator, 2026-11-02 04:00 UTC; revised 04:10 UTC on resumption in the project "Midterms" (items 10, 12, 16). A brief cites this file rather than restating it, so that a change here reaches every worker at once. Every rule carries its reason or points to one; if you find one that does not, say so in your report (`CLAUDE.md`).*

1. **Read first, whole and in this order:** `CLAUDE.md`, `LAW.md`, `the-law-for-agents.md`, `RULINGS.md`, `STATE.md`, this file, then your brief. *Reason:* the law's words alone carry its meaning (general law 7c); a summary of it is someone else's reading.
2. **Your brief is your law** (general law 13a): it states your sub-end and why it is a condition of the site's end. Where your brief is unclear on a small point, read it the way that best serves your sub-end; where the sub-end does not settle it, escalate to the orchestrator (1b). *Reason:* an agent fills a gap with a confident guess unless it is told where to look.
3. **You choose nothing for the site.** You bring candidates, each with what it gives toward the end and the four measures (law 1a: speed, information, ease, beauty), its costs, its licence and the evidence. The orchestrator chooses, and the choices return to the maker in the plan. *Reason:* law 6b and 6a; general law 14b.
4. **Breadth first, then narrow.** First what kinds of thing exist and where they are catalogued; then the items; then depth on the few that could fit. *Reason:* general law 12a: a model is confident on little context and cannot know, without searching, whether more exists.
5. **Inherited evidence is checked, not repeated blindly, and does not limit you.** `inherited/README.md` names it. Go where it did not. *Reason:* the maker's ruling of 2026-11-02 00:05 UTC (`RULINGS.md`).
6. **Mark every fact** **[measured]** (you observed it), **[stated]** (the source says so, with where), **[vendor claim]**, or **[inferred]** (your reading). *Reason:* law 7: the orchestrator must know which facts are true things from true sources and which are guesses.
7. **Web pages:** `curl -sSL` into a scratch file outside the repository, then search it; prefer `.md` or `llms.txt`; a shallow `git clone` into scratch for a repository; WebFetch only when curl is refused, and say so. Read a deciding fact (a price, a licence term, a poll-closing hour, a field in a feed) at its source, whole. A page behind a bot check is recorded as unread and not retried or circumvented. *Reason:* `CLAUDE.md`; WebFetch returns a small model's summary; the survey worker's retry of a bot check was refused by the session's safety layer (inherited S1), and getting around a site's refusal is not ours to do.

Paid options: find every published price and plan, read at the seller's page; where a price is not published, say what contact would be needed and how long a key is said to take, and **do not contact the seller**. No message to any outside party, no post anywhere, no purchase. *Reason:* law 7a; a message to a seller is a message in the maker's name.
