*Brief written by the orchestrator, 2026-11-02 11:39 UTC, on the maker's ruling of 11:36 UTC (`RULINGS.md`). Read `briefs/_COMMON.md` first; it is part of this brief, except items 3 and 12 where this brief says otherwise.*

**Model and effort: Opus 5.5, high.** *Reason:* whether a motion serves a surface's purpose is judgment (law 4, 5; `OPERATIONS.md` 4), and the maker named motion as the one part of the plan he does not approve.

## Your sub-end

The builders (W9 and the agents after it) can give every moving surface of the site the motion that serves that surface's purpose, or no motion where none serves it, from a written specification grounded in evidence: for each surface, what it is for, what real event (if any) it shows, whether it moves, how, for how long and with what curve, on desktop and on phone, and what happens when a viewer asks for reduced motion.

*Why this is a condition of the end:* the maker's ruling: "Thoroughly research motion for agents, as I am very concerned that a single kind of motion applied everywhere will not work. Motion should satisfy its surface's purpose, not be there just because." Law 4 asks that ways of moving be chosen deliberately for each kind of screen; law 5 that motion be exact in timing and natural in feel, and that motion which signals an event is information only when the event is real. The plan's line "Every motion uses that one spring" was not approved; it is the case he names.

- **Purpose first.** For each surface, state its purpose for the room watching results before any motion; a motion that serves no stated purpose is dropped, however fine it looks. Motion that implies an event must only run on a real event (a new count, a call, a withdrawal, a viewer's own action); nothing moves on a timer or for decoration. *Reason:* the ruling's words; law 5.
- **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`** and the access guidance on motion (WCAG 2.3.3 and the vestibular literature), read at the source. *Reason:* the site is open to anyone with the address (the maker's 03:15 UTC settlement); a motion that sickens a viewer fails ease (law 1a).
- **Weight:** anything you propose must fit W6's budget (first view about 300 KB compressed on a phone, JavaScript about 100 KB; `research/stack/` s.6) and run at the display's frame rate on a mid-range phone; measure it. *Reason:* law 2 and 4; a dropped frame reads as a fault.
- **You write the specification; the orchestrator adopts it** (in place of `_COMMON.md` 3: you recommend one choice per surface, with the evidence and the rejected alternatives, rather than an open list). *Reason:* the build is running against the clock (law 6) and the orchestrator holds the choice (law 6b); a recommendation with its evidence lets it adopt quickly, and anything it cannot settle it escalates.
