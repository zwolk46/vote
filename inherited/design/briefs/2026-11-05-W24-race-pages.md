   - a race page (`/ak/senate`, `/ny/house-17`), covering a Senate seat, a House district, a governor, a measure, a state legislative seat and a local office, each before results, counting, called, and with a changed call;

2. **Order and hierarchy.** For each kind of page, put the information in order of what the reader came for. That usually means: who is winning or won, by how much, how much is counted, and who called it. Then the detail: the counties or map, the timeline, the markets, the candidates' records, and the sources. Follow the visual hierarchy principles in the design record. Show the plan to the orchestrator before building, as W22 did. Reason: the maker approves direction through the orchestrator, and a plan is cheaper to change than a build.

   - Faces sit centred on one column across lines.
   - Status lines ("Polls close", "CNN call…") get their own line box, never overlapping.
   - Use the face-in-line rule from #167 (W23); don't invent another.
4. **Long pages** get the same sticky table of sections as the home page (reuse W22's `Sections.jsx` and `spy.js`).
5. **Surprise.** W21 is building a per-race "how surprising was this" component (Zach, 20:47). Leave a place for it in the race page's order, and place it when it lands.
